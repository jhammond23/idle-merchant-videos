In this town, there's a castle on the hill with a hundred doors.
Behind every door is a trial. Pass it, and the next door opens.
And whoever opens door one hundred gets one wish. Any wish.
But there's one rule nobody says out loud.
If you fail a trial, nothing hurts. You just turn to stone. Crack! One more statue in the castle garden.
And some of them still look surprised.
That's why the garden gets a little more crowded every year.
My dad has been stuck in bed since the first snow. The healers say no potion in the kingdom can fix him.
But any wish means any wish.
So the morning I turned eleven, I packed half a loaf of bread, grabbed Dad's old wooden sword, and walked up the hill.
My little sister held my hand at the gate. "You can do this. For Dad," she said.
Then the gate slammed shut behind me. Boom!
Door one was a bridge of floating stones over a hole with no bottom.
Every stone sank the second you stood on it. Bad news, because I have never stood still in my life. Just ask my teacher.
I hopped. I slipped. I almost fell, but I made it.
A bell rang. "Gift earned. Feather Step."
Door seven was worse. A suit of armor the size of a barn woke up and chased me through a maze.
Clank. Clank. Clank.
I ran faster than I'd ever run. Even faster than when my sister yells, "Last one home does the dishes!"
Ding! "Gift earned. Swift."
I kept going. Door thirty. Door forty-one. Door forty-nine.
Then came door fifty, and someone was already there. Waiting.
He wore a black cloak covered in hundreds of tiny keys, all jingling.
"Another climber!" he laughed. "I'm the Key Thief. I don't pass trials. I skip them."
He turned a golden key in the air, and the floor under me opened.
Feather Step! I floated right over the hole.
Then he unlocked a door in thin air and stepped out behind me.
But this time, I was faster. Swift! I was already behind him.
He turned the key one last time. "Lock."
My feet went cold and gray. I was turning to stone.
I thought about my sister at the gate. You can do this. For Dad.
That's when I understood.
He never opened a single door the real way. He never passed one trial.
So he never earned one real gift. All he had was that key.
My legs were stone, but my arms still worked.
Giant Grip! I grabbed that golden key and snapped it in half.
Crack! Every key on his cloak crumbled to dust. The thief screamed and dropped to his knees.
My legs turned back to normal. I won! I actually won!
Far below the window, I could see our farm. The candle in Dad's window was still lit. My sister was still sitting on the fence, waiting.
Then the thief started laughing. "You think I'm the worst thing in this castle?"
The torches above us went out. One. By. One.
Then the doors upstairs started slamming open. Bang. Bang. Bang.
Sixty. Eighty. One hundred.
Something was coming down the stairs. I couldn't see a face. Only a golden crown, glowing in the dark.
"The wish is mine," it said.
The Key Thief was never the boss of this castle. He was just the king's guard dog.
