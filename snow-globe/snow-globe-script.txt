In this town, the sky is glass, and a giant eye is always watching.
The king said a monster lived up there, so small boys like me must never look up.
Whenever the town shook like a jar of marbles, he blamed the monster.
And my little sister hid under her bed.
So I ran up the bell tower and looked up anyway.
There was no monster, only glass as thin as a soap bubble.
I pushed through, and I learned the secret.
That glass makes everything inside it small.
Outside it, I grew until my town was smaller than my shoe.
We lived inside a snow globe, and the wind kept sliding it toward the edge of a shelf.
One more gust tipped it over the edge, and I caught it.
My whole town fit in one hand, like a snowy cupcake.
The king looked up, saw a giant eye, and screamed, "Monster!"
It was me, the boy he called small. Now he was shorter than my fingernail.
I was never small. I was just on the wrong side of the glass.
Now every evening, I press my eye to the glass.
