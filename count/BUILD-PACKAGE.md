1. **No Fold.** The lead is an ordinary kid.
2. **No question and no A/B vote at the end.** The story ends on its last line, which is a twist.

**About this story:** A creepy town-rule story. The guard counts everyone at sunset, and one too many means the last one counted is put outside the wall. The narrator is a kid who counts everything, and that habit is how he wins and how the last line lands.

## The look
- **One continuous moving camera (Jake, 11-22: use this on every video from now on, the way The String was filmed).** No hard cuts for at least 80% of the running time. The camera never stops: it glides and sways, rises over the roofs, drops back to eye level, pushes in on faces and pulls back for the big pictures. Where a beat below names a new picture, fly the camera to it instead of cutting.
- **Keep the rhythm uneven.** Jake's note: the successful videos have no set time between scenes. Snap to a new framing in under half a second on lists, jokes and action, and drift slowly for 3 to 4 seconds on the big pictures (marked HOLD in the beats). No fixed beat.
- Clear, readable light in every shot. Sunset and night scenes are allowed here, but they must be warm or moonlit and sharp: faces and the key object always clearly lit. No fog and no murky grade.
- Huge captions: white capitals with a thick black outline, 2-3 words at a time, centered low, matching the voice word for word. The last two lines are spoken quietly, so their captions matter most: show every word.
- The @idlemerchantrng watermark sits in a CORNER for the whole video.
- Music: low and uneasy under the voice, and it should drop out for the last line.
- **End card:** after the last line, hold the final picture for about one second, then end with the standard 5-second "Use Flashloop.AI, creator code RNG" card, the same one that closes my-rock-hatched-a-dragon. That is the only promo allowed. Nothing promotional before it.
- Kid-safe: nobody is shown being hurt, no blood, no body. The scares are what we do not see.

## The lead: Tam
Tam is an ordinary kid, about eleven, plain patched clothes from the poor end of town. No powers. He counts on his fingers under his breath: give him a small tally-mark habit (chalk marks on a wall, fingers ticking). Build him from the Lunchbox cast parts. Do not use Fold in this video.

## Script (final, word for word)
In this town, the night guard counts us every sunset. Yesterday there were two hundred of us. Tonight he counted two hundred and one, and he's looking at me.
Nothing outside can climb our wall. But a long time ago, something walked in through the gate, wearing somebody's face. So we count.
The rule is simple. One too many, and the last one counted goes outside the wall until morning.
Nobody who went out has ever come back in.
Last winter it was old Mrs. Pell. She walked out with a blanket and a sandwich.
Tonight, somebody handed me a blanket.
The guard is Captain Brill. He counts on his fingers. Twice he's had to take his boots off.
And somehow, the last one he counts is always from the poor end of town. I live at the poor end.
But my name is Tam, and I count everything. Stairs. Peas. My best friend Mabel's freckles. Sixty one. Always sixty one.
And tonight I counted us. Two hundred. I counted again. I always count again. Two hundred.
So Brill wasn't miscounting. He was choosing.
I said, "Count again."
He said, "I never miscount."
I said, "Then count yourself." And I pointed at him. "Two hundred and one."
The whole square went quiet. In my whole life, nobody had ever counted the captain.
Brill smiled. "I'm the guard, boy."
I said, "Then come out of the shade."
Because I count that too. Every sunset of my life, he has stood in the shadow of the gate. Every single one.
Everybody looked at him. He stepped out.
The sun was low. Every one of us had a shadow as long as a street.
Captain Brill had nothing under his boots.
He stopped smiling. His face slid a little, like a hat that doesn't fit.
The rule is the rule. The last one counted goes outside the wall.
It took forty of us to push him through the gate. He was very heavy, and he was not shaped like a man under that coat.
We locked it. The gate was open for fifty one seconds. I counted. Then I gave the blanket back.
Then something knocked. Three times, very politely.
And Mrs. Pell's voice said, "Let me in, dear. It's cold."
Nobody opened it.
That night I counted us, the way I always do. Two hundred. Perfect.
Then I counted shadows. I'd never thought to, before today.
One hundred and ninety nine.
Mabel was right next to me, in the lamplight, holding my hand.
So I counted her freckles.
Sixty.

## Other characters and things
- **Captain Brill:** the night guard. Big, long heavy coat down to his boots, friendly smile. Until line 19 he ALWAYS stands inside the dark shadow of the gate arch, so we never see whether he has a shadow. After he steps out: no shadow at all, and his face slips slightly sideways like a mask.
- **Mabel:** Tam's best friend, same age, a face covered in freckles (make them big enough to read on a phone). She must appear next to Tam in the early crowd shots so viewers know her.
- **Mrs. Pell:** a small old woman with a blanket and a sandwich, seen once walking out of the gate in winter. Later we only hear about her voice: show the closed gate, never her.
- **The town:** a walled medieval town with one big gate and a square in front of it. About two hundred people in the square at sunset.
