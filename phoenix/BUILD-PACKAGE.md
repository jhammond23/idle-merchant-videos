## The vibe (read this before anything else)
**Awe, then injustice, then triumph. Play it completely straight. It is not a comedy.**
- **Light tells the whole story.** Warm candle gold for the ceremony. Then blackness. Then one red glow far away. Then a bird that is the only light in the room, relighting the hall candle by candle as it flies. By the end the whole hall is gold and the dead fireplace is roaring. Deep black shadows throughout, but faces must always read.
- **The boy is small, quiet and grey.** Soot on his face and hands, ash in his hair, bare feet, a dented bucket. He never poses or shouts. He is the dullest thing in a bright room until the fire reaches him, and then he is the only thing you look at. Keep him tiny in the frame until he takes the feather.
- **The Phoenix is slow, huge and calm.** Every wingbeat is an event. It never hurries, never screeches, never attacks anyone. It is gentle with the boy and ignores everyone else.
- **The room does the reacting:** the laughing court, the royal children ducking, the hunting birds hiding their heads, the Prince's smile dying with his glove still in the air, a hundred guards with buckets, then every bird in the hall bowing.
- **The Prince is smug and golden. The King is cold and never surprised.**
- **Sound:** harps and chatter for the ceremony; total silence when the candles die; a low furnace roar as it rises; wingbeats like a big sail filling; each candle relighting with a soft pop; a hard half-second of silence before the bow; one deep bass note on the bow; a cold string under the King; a rising warm swell on the feather; a great hiss of steam. No looping music bed: Jake asked on 11-23 for sound effects instead of a looping track on this kind of story.
- **Pace:** quick, clean cuts for the first twenty seconds. After that, long smooth gliding moves with three-to-four second holds on the big pictures: the wings opening, the flight, the bow, the feather.
- Kid-safe: nobody is hurt, nobody fights, the fire never burns anyone, and the water only makes steam.

## Jake's camera rule (new today, please follow it exactly)
Jake watched the Black Knight one-take today and said the fast camera swings at the start "keep jerking around" and would make people leave. So:
- **The first 24 seconds are steady shots joined by hard cuts.** No whips, no fast swings, no snap zooms anywhere in the film.
- **From 24 seconds on it is smooth continuous camera:** slow glides, slow rises, one slow even circle. If a move would need to be fast to work, make it a cut instead.
- Hard cuts are allowed only where the beats say HARD CUT: nine in the story, plus the cut into the sponsor ending.

## Cast and set
Use the Lunchbox faces, hair and outfits. **Do not reuse the Black Knight's court as it is:** that video is going out again this week, and this must look like a different kingdom. Give this court white, gold and deep green instead of red and gold.
- **Me:** about ten. A thin ash boy in a grey-brown tunic, bare feet, soot on his face and hands, grey ash in his hair. He holds a dented iron bucket with a short hearth brush in every shot until he takes the feather.
- **The Phoenix:** see CREATURE-PHOENIX.md.
- **The Prince:** about twelve, fair hair, white and gold clothes, one long golden falconer's glove. Smug.
- **The Golden Eagle:** his bird, with a small jewelled hood.
- **The King:** tall, dark beard, crown, a deep green and gold robe, a cold face.
- **The room:** about twenty royal children, each with a falcon or hawk on a glove; falconers; the court; a hundred guards with buckets.
- **The great hall:** very long, stone pillars, green and gold banners, a pale carpet from the great doors to the throne, and forty tall candle stands along it in pairs. **Behind the throne, a huge stone fireplace as tall as two men: cold, grey and full of old ash, with a worn carving of a firebird over a crowned king above it.** By the doors, a small servants' hearth where the boy works. It has to work as one space the camera can glide through from end to end and up to the ceiling.

## Script (final, word for word)
In this town, the Phoenix bowed to me.
Every royal kid gets a hunting bird on Hawking Day.
I'm the ash boy who cleans their fireplaces, so I watch from the back with my bucket.
This year, the Prince got the Golden Eagle. Nobody had seen one in ten years.
Then he pointed at me and said, "Keep sweeping, ash boy. Maybe one day a pigeon will pick you."
The whole hall laughed, even the King.
Then every candle in the hall went out.
The cold fireplace behind the throne began to glow, and something stood up in the ashes with wings as wide as the hall.
It was the Phoenix, and nobody had seen it in a thousand years.
It flew the length of the hall, and every candle it passed lit itself again.
It flew over the royal kids, and their birds hid their heads.
The Prince held up his golden glove, like the Phoenix was coming for him.
The Phoenix flew right past him.
It flew all the way to the back of the hall, all the way to me.
One step away, it landed.
Then it folded its wings and bowed its head to the floor.
The whole hall gasped, but the King didn't.
He stood up and said, "I knew it would come back for him."
The old story says the Phoenix only rises for the next true king.
Ten years ago, the King took the throne from my real father.
Then he hid the baby prince where a phoenix would never look.
It would look for a prince, not a boy covered in soot.
Then the King shouted, "Put it out!"
A hundred guards ran in with buckets of water, and the doors slammed shut behind us.
The Phoenix didn't fight.
It pulled one burning feather from its own wing and held it out to me.
It should have burned me, but it felt as warm as my bucket of ashes.
I lifted it over my head, and every bird in the hall left its royal kid.
The falcons, the hawks, and even the Golden Eagle landed around me and bowed at once.
A hundred buckets of water turned to steam.
The Prince was the only one left holding up a glove, next to his father.
The King spent ten years hiding me in the ashes.
He forgot where a phoenix comes from.
What would you name your phoenix?

## The planted clues and their payoffs
- **The cold fireplace** behind the throne is in frame every time we see the King before the candles go out. It is roaring in the second-to-last beat.
- **The carving** of the firebird over a king is seen dimly at 21 s and lit at 66 s.
- **The bucket:** he holds it in every shot until the feather. "It felt as warm as my bucket of ashes." Frame his grip on the feather the same way as his grip on the handle.
- **The golden glove:** raised at 45 s, still raised and empty at 108 s.
- **The ashes:** he is covered in them from his first shot. The last two lines are about them.
