# Creature build sheet: THE PHOENIX (main character of "The Phoenix Bowed to Me")

## What it is
The last Phoenix: a giant bird of living fire that has not been seen in a thousand years. The old story says it only rises when it is time for the next true king to rule. It rises out of the cold ashes of the great fireplace behind the throne, flies the length of the hall past every royal child, and bows to the soot-covered ash boy at the back. It must look like the most powerful thing anyone in the room has ever seen, and then be gentle with him. Not cute, not a monster: a king's bird.

## The one-second read (most important)
On a phone, with no sound, a stranger should see: a huge bird made of fire, wings spread wider than a throne room, lighting up a dark hall. It is its own light source. Protect that picture in every shot.

## Size
- Body about the size of a warhorse. Wingspan about 20 metres: when the wings are open they nearly touch both walls of the hall.
- The ash boy (about 1.4 m) stands lower than its chest. When it bows, its head comes down to his feet.

## Look
- A noble raptor shape: a strong hooked beak, a long neck, a long sweeping tail. Think eagle crossed with peacock, built for a blocky Roblox-style world.
- Feathers run from white-gold at the chest and the leading edge of the wings, through orange and deep red, to charcoal-black tips with glowing cracks, like coals. The body gives off real light that falls on the floor, the pillars and people's faces.
- A crest of tall flame feathers on the head that reads as a crown.
- A tail like a comet: long ember feathers that leave a trail of sparks and a thin ribbon of smoke.
- Eyes of molten gold, calm and steady. A real readable face on a phone.
- Talons and beak like dark iron.
- Every wingbeat throws a soft wave of sparks and a gust that moves hair, banners and candle flames.

## Three looks
1. Rising: fierce, head high, wings opening.
2. Soft: when it bows to the boy.
3. Protective glare: head low and forward, wings half open around him like a wall of fire.

## Moves needed
1. **Rise from the ashes:** a pile of grey ash glows from inside, embers lift and gather, and it stands up and opens its wings to full span. This is the big entrance.
2. **Slow heavy flight** down a long hall, a little above head height, each wingbeat lighting the candles it passes.
3. **Glide past** a row of children holding small hunting birds, and past a prince holding up a golden glove, without slowing.
4. **Landing** one step from the boy: wings flare, sparks roll across the floor.
5. **The bow:** wings fold, the long neck curves down, and the head touches the floor at the boy's feet. Hold it.
6. **The feather:** it pulls one burning feather from its own wing with its beak and holds it out to him.
7. **Protective stance:** standing behind the boy with wings half open around him.
8. **Idle:** breathing, feathers flickering, tail drifting.

## Separate props
- **The burning feather:** about as long as the boy's arm, bright as a torch, a separate piece he can hold over his head. It must light his face.
- **The ash bucket:** a dented iron bucket half full of grey ash with a short hearth brush. He carries it in every shot until he takes the feather.

## Also useful (smaller, if time allows)
- **The Golden Eagle:** the Prince's bird, proud, gold-feathered, wearing a small jewelled hood. It must be able to sit on a glove, hide its head under a wing, and bow on the floor.
- A simple falcon and hawk that can do the same three things, for the other royal children.

## Sound
- Rising: a deep whoomph like a furnace door opening, then a low roar that settles into a steady crackle.
- Wingbeats: like a big sail filling with wind.
- The bow: the fire sound drops almost to silence.

## Colour and light
- White-gold, orange, red and charcoal. No blue anywhere.
- It has to work in a pitch-dark hall (it is the only light) and in a hall full of candlelight. Keep the face readable in both: do not let it blow out to plain white.

## Original design only
Do not base it on any existing game, film or show character.
