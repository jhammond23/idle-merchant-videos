Channel: "In this town" story reels for Idle Merchant RNG (@idlemerchantrng) on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube. Format: vertical 1080x1920, 30 fps, Roblox-style blocky 3D characters, bold burned-in captions.

**This batch is a style test (Jake, 11-22).** The biggest "in this town" accounts were studied this morning, and these scripts follow what their hits share. So this package differs from earlier ones in five ways:
1. **No Fold.** The lead is an ordinary kid.
2. **No question and no A/B vote at the end.** The story ends on its last line, which is a twist.
3. **The voice is faster** (about 3.9 words a second). It is final. Do not speed it up again.
4. **Shot length must vary.** See "The look".
5. **Title-only caption** when it is posted (Claude handles posting).

**About this story:** A 35-second mini-story with one idea and one twist. It should feel fast and quiet. Every picture is footprints.

## The look
- **Vary the shot length. Do not cut on a fixed beat.** Jake's note: the successful videos have no set time between scenes. Use quick cuts of about 1 second for lists, jokes and action, and hold for 3 to 4 seconds on the big pictures (marked HOLD in the beats). Never hold a still frame: on a hold, the camera drifts or pushes in.
- Clear, readable light in every shot. Sunset and night scenes are allowed here, but they must be warm or moonlit and sharp: faces and the key object always clearly lit. No fog and no murky grade.
- Huge captions: white capitals with a thick black outline, 2-3 words at a time, centered low, matching the voice word for word. The last two lines are spoken quietly, so their captions matter most: show every word.
- The @idlemerchantrng watermark sits in a CORNER for the whole video.
- Music: low and uneasy under the voice, and it should drop out for the last line.
- **End card:** after the last line, hold the final picture for about one second, then end with the standard 5-second "Use Flashloop.AI, creator code RNG" card, the same one that closes my-rock-hatched-a-dragon. That is the only promo allowed. Nothing promotional before it.
- Kid-safe: nobody is shown being hurt, no blood, no body. The scares are what we do not see.

## The lead: The kid
The kid is an ordinary kid, about eleven, plain clothes, never named. We mostly see him from behind or at his bedroom window. Build him from the Lunchbox cast parts. Do not use Fold in this video.

## Script (final, word for word)
In this town, your footprints show up the day before you make them. This morning, mine walk out my front door, down to the river, and stop.
Everybody checks theirs at breakfast. The baker's go to the pub. Every single day. He acts surprised.
Mine have never gone anywhere. School. Home. School. Until today.
So tomorrow I'm not going anywhere. I've locked the door. I'm staying in bed.
It's dark now. I just looked out the window, to see if mine were gone.
They're still there.
But there's a second set now. Bigger. Bare.
They come up out of the river, all the way to my front door.
Then they go back down, right beside mine.
And from the door on, mine aren't footsteps anymore.
They're two long lines, side by side, all the way down to the water.

## Other characters and things
- **Tomorrow's footprints:** the key picture. Every person in town has a trail of softly glowing pale-blue footprints on the ground showing where they will walk tomorrow. They must read clearly on a phone.
- **The baker:** a round man whose glowing footprints lead from his shop straight into the pub. He throws up his hands in fake surprise.
- **The second set:** huge, bare, wet footprints, three times the size of the kid's, glowing the same blue. Never show what makes them.
- **The river:** at the bottom of the hill below the kid's house, dark and wide.
