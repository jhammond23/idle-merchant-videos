In this town, the dungeon under the castle picks one master every thousand years.
This year, it picked the torch boy. Me.
The dungeon has nine floors of monsters. The prince went down every summer with fifty knights, and his record was floor three.
I was the kid who carried their torches.
And every night, I left one torch burning at the dungeon gate, because I didn't like the idea of anything sitting alone in the dark.
This summer, he took us down to floor four, and the walls started to shake. The knights ran, and the prince left me behind.
"Keep the monsters busy, torch boy."
Then my torch went out.
A second later, every torch on all nine floors lit up at once, and a deep voice came out of the walls. It said, "Master."
I didn't know what else to say, so I tried, "Stairs."
The wall in front of me folded open like a paper fan.
Then I tried, "Ride."
A dragon the size of the church walked out of the dark and lay down at my feet, like a dog waiting for a walk.
I found the prince on floor three, surrounded by goblins.
The goblins took one look at me and stepped aside like a curtain.
The prince whispered, "What are you?"
I said, "You brought fifty knights and made it to floor three. I brought one torch, and now I own all nine."
Then I rode the dragon up the stairs and into the middle of the town square.
The whole square went silent.
The prince came out behind me, carried by three goblins, because his legs had stopped working.
Everyone asked why the dungeon would choose a torch boy. But I knew. For six years, I was the only one who left a light on for it.
Even a dungeon remembers who was kind to it in the dark.
The next morning, the prince knocked on my door and asked if I needed someone to carry my torches.
I told him I'd think about it.
Would you let him carry them? A, yes. B, never.
