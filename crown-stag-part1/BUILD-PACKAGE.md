## The vibe (read this before anything else)
**Dark medieval fantasy, played straight. The feeling is awe: the biggest, most feared thing in the world chooses the smallest person on the field.** Think of a war legend told by the kid it happened to, years later, with a dry sense of humour. It is not a comedy and it is not a horror.

- **Dark in design, bright in light.** The darkness comes from what things are made of: a black stag, iron, mud, torn war banners, a castle built out of stolen thrones. The lighting stays bright and golden so everything reads on a phone. Never underexpose. Frame 0 must be bright.
- **The kid is small, quiet and steady.** He never poses, never shouts, never raises his arms in triumph. He stands still while everyone else runs. That stillness is his whole power, and Part 2 says so out loud. Keep him tiny in the frame next to the stag for as long as possible.
- **The Kingtaker (the stag) is slow, heavy and silent.** Every step is an event. He looks like the end of the world from far away and like a tired old soldier up close. His eyes do the acting: a hard war stare, then soft when he kneels, then a protective glare.
- **Scale is the spectacle.** Always keep something small in the frame to measure against: the kid, the church, a horse, a soldier. Use low angles looking up. When the camera rides up with the kid, the audience should feel the height in their stomach.
- **The crowns are the treasure.** Ninety-nine gold crowns swinging in black antlers is the picture people will remember. Give them real sparkle: sun glints, small lens flares, a soft chime when they move. When they fall, they ring.
- **The humour is dry and tiny.** The goat in a little helmet, a crown too big for the kid's head, "I nodded. I had no idea." Play each one completely straight and move on in under two seconds. Nobody mugs for the camera.
- **The enemy king is cold, calm and thin.** He never shouts in Part 1. He is the only person in the story who is not surprised. He has no crown on his head.
- **Pace:** uneven on purpose. Fast when people run, when the stag charges and on whips. Long slow drifts of three to four seconds on the big pictures: the antlers over the tree line, the ride up, the castle of thrones, the mountain standing. The slow shots are where the awe lives, so do not rush them.
- **Colour:** black, bone-white and gold, with red war banners. Warm golden daylight. Keep blue sky to a small part of the frame (use haze, low sun, dust and mist) so the cover is gold and black, not blue.
- Kid-safe: nobody is hurt, nobody fights, no blood. Beasts and soldiers kneel, run or fall over.

## The story in three sentences
In this town, wars are settled by one war beast from each side, and the winner keeps the losing town's crown. The enemy's beast, a giant black stag carrying ninety-nine crowns, comes for ours, our goat and our army run, and I stay where I am, so the stag kneels to me, carries me to his king, and gives me all ninety-nine crowns. Then the king, who holds far more, wakes up his first war beast, which is the mountain behind his castle.

## The world (one valley, built once, used by both parts)
It must work as **one continuous place** so the camera can travel from the war field, over the river, to the castle, and up the mountain without cutting.

1. **Our town (near side).** Small and poor and warm: timber and thatch, a low stone wall, one stone church with a short square tower (the tower is the measuring stick for "taller than our church"). Cream and green banners, patched. A barn at the edge of the field with a half-open door.
2. **The war field (between the towns).** A wide trampled meadow with old ruts and puddles, morning mist lying low, two rows of banner poles facing each other. On our side stands a single pole with **our town's crown** on top: small, iron and brass, a bit bent. Every town in this world keeps its crown on a pole like this.
3. **The river.** Shallow, wide and bright, with a low stone bridge that looks like a toy next to the stag.
4. **The enemy town and the king's castle (far side).** Black and red banners, sharp roofs. The castle is **built out of thrones**: thousands of chairs of every kind (carved wood, stone, iron, gold, velvet gone grey) stacked and mortared into walls and towers. From far away it is just a jagged dark castle. At middle distance the walls look oddly bumpy. Up close you see chair backs, armrests and lion-paw feet. The gate is two giant throne backs. On the wall above the gate is the king's balcony with one small cushioned chair, a telescope and a little table with a cup on it (this matters in Part 2: he watches wars from here and never comes down).
5. **The mountain behind the castle.** A long forested hill. It is really the king's first war beast lying asleep: its outline should be just barely animal-shaped (a long back, a hump for the shoulder, a lower rise for the head), and the "forest" on top is a tangle of huge grey dead branches that are its antlers. Thousands of tiny gold points glint in those branches from the first time we see it. Nobody should notice on first watch. Everybody should notice on the second.

**Light across the story:** Part 1 starts in bright golden early morning with mist, becomes clear late morning for the ride, turns cooler and redder under the castle walls, and ends in the mountain's shadow (a sudden eclipse-like shade with gold glints, still readable). Part 2 starts in that shadow, breaks back into gold when the crowns fall, and ends in a warm orange sunset and lantern-lit dusk.

## Cast
Use the Lunchbox faces, hair and outfits as usual.
- **Me:** about eleven. Small and thin, mud-brown tunic, rope belt, bare shins, messy hair. He is the kid who looks after our war beast, so he starts the story **holding the goat's rope**. After the goat runs he is left holding the empty rope, and he keeps it in his hand for the whole of Part 1. From 27 seconds on he wears one gold crown that is too big for him and sits crooked over one eyebrow. He pushes it up with one finger now and then.
- **Our war beast, the goat:** scruffy grey-white, one bent horn, wearing a tiny dented war helmet and a little cream-and-green banner on its back. Played straight. If the Lunchbox catalog has no goat, model a simple one.
- **Our army:** about thirty farmers with pitchforks and cooking-pot helmets.
- **The Kingtaker:** the Crown Stag. See `CREATURE-CROWN-STAG.md`.
- **The enemy army:** hundreds, in black and red, with pikes and armoured warhorses.
- **The enemy general:** big, loud, red plume, on the biggest horse.
- **The enemy king:** tall, thin, pale, sharp-faced, about fifty. Black and gold robes with a fur collar. **No crown.** Grey hair combed flat. Hands behind his back.
- **The Mountain:** the king's first war beast: the Crown Stag's shape at mountain size (the "later variant" in the creature file). Mossy stone-grey coat with earth and small trees still clinging to its back, antlers like a dead forest, thousands of crowns. Its eyes are two huge slow gold lamps. It only has to stand up in Part 1. In Part 2 it raises a hoof, kneels, lays its neck down and walks.
- **A few ordinary war beasts** for the first seconds (each about the size of a horse or a bear): a boar with two crowns hooked on its tusks, a bear with three on a chain round its neck, a rooster the size of a pony with one on its head. They also return at the end of Part 2.

Scale cheats are fine (forced perspective, a scaled-up mesh, instanced crowns, a painted far background) as long as the size reads on a phone.

## The camera plan
- **One moving take** from the first hard cut to the kneel, and again from the kneel to the end. The camera glides, tracks, whips, climbs and cranes. It does not cut.
- **Hard cuts are allowed only where the beats say HARD CUT: seven at most**, plus the cut into the sponsor ending. They are: the jump from the cover picture to the start of the morning; the three impact cuts on the kneel; in and out of the one-second goat flash; and, only if needed, the cut to a wide shot when the Mountain stands.
- **Other techniques, each used once:** a camera that stays dead still while the army runs past it; half a second of total silence before the kneel; the kid's glance into the lens on "You'd climb up, right?"; slow motion on the crowns sliding down; the long push-in that turns castle walls into thrones.
- **Height is the main tool.** Low and looking up for the stag. Rising with the kid for the ride. Tilting up and never quite reaching the top for the Mountain.
- At least 85% of the running time must be continuous camera.

## Sound
- **Under everything:** a low war drum, slow, like a heartbeat, and a dark drone. Wind on the field.
- **The stag:** one deep thud per step with a small ground shake, a soft chime of crowns every time he moves, one long low breath before he kneels.
- **Silence is a tool.** Cut the music and the room sound to nothing for about half a second right before each kneel.
- **The kneel:** a deep bass hit when the knees land. It should be the loudest moment so far.
- **Crowns falling:** ringing gold, like a hundred small bells. This is the treasure sound; make it beautiful.
- **The king:** a single cold string note whenever he speaks.
- **The Mountain:** a sound from far underground, like a ship's horn mixed with a landslide.
- **Reward moments** (the ride up, the crowns sliding down): the music opens into a warm rising swell with a low choir.
- The voice must stay clear on top of all of it.

## Script (final, word for word)
In this town, we don't fight wars. Our war beasts do, and the winner keeps the loser's crown.
Most beasts carry two or three.
The black stag across the river is taller than our church. It carries ninety-nine on its antlers.
Lose your crown, and their king owns your town. Today it came for ours.
Our war beast is a goat. He ran. So did our army. I didn't.
And in front of both towns, their war beast knelt. To me.
Their soldiers froze. Their horses knelt too. Then one crown slid off its antlers and landed on my head.
Then it laid its neck down like a ramp. You'd climb up, right? So did I.
It stood up, and their whole army looked like toy soldiers.
Their general yelled, "The Kingtaker belongs to our king!"
"Good," I said. "I'll bring him back myself."
We crossed the river in one step.
The king's castle was built out of thrones, one from every town he ever took.
But the Kingtaker only won ninety-nine.
Then the king walked out, alone. Nobody has ever seen what won him the rest.
"He won me ninety-nine crowns," the king said, "and he never once knelt to me."
"A war beast only kneels to its true king. You know what that means."
I nodded. I had no idea.
"It means those crowns are yours," he said.
The Kingtaker lowered his head, and all ninety-nine crowns slid down to my feet.
That morning, I owned a goat. Now I owned ninety-nine towns.
"In this kingdom, whoever holds the most crowns is king," he said. "And I still hold more than you."
Then the ground shook. The mountain behind his castle stood up.
It was his first war beast. The forest on its head was antlers, and every branch held a crown.
His army knelt. The Kingtaker didn't. He stepped in front of me.
How many crowns are in that forest? Guess.
Part 2 is on my profile.
