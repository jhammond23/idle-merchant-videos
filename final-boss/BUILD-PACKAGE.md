# Build package: I'M THE FINAL BOSS OF THIS TOWN

**Jake wants this one posted on Wednesday 11-25.** Build it straight after dungeon-master.

Channel: "In this town" story reels for Idle Merchant RNG (@idlemerchantrng) on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube. The audience is mostly young adult men who like fantasy and games. Format: vertical 1080x1920, 30 fps, Roblox-style blocky 3D characters, bold burned-in captions.

**About this story:** it grants a wish. The viewer should want to be this kid: the priest reads his class and it is Final Boss, four hundred heroes come to beat him, and he cannot be bothered to stand up. He turns the whole attack into a business for his mom's bakery. Jake approved every word by voice on 11-22 and asked for it to be as unpredictable as possible. It is short on purpose (about a minute) and has no question or vote at the end.

## The vibe (new section, asked for by Jake on 11-22: read this before anything else)
Jake's words for this style: **"never let them know your next move."** It is deadpan comedy about a kid with far too much power.
- **The kid is never surprised, never angry and never in a hurry.** Small calm face, half-closed eyes, hands in pockets or doing something ordinary (sweeping, yawning, eating). He does not pose, flex or shout. The less he reacts, the funnier and the more powerful he looks.
- **Everyone else overreacts.** Kings, princes, heroes and zombies get the big faces: jaws dropping, eyes popping, arms flailing, running in circles. All the energy in the frame comes from them.
- **Joke timing: say it, then show it.** Each joke is two beats: the setup picture, then a quick snap (under half a second) to the surprise picture exactly as the punchline word is spoken. Never show the surprise before the word. Then hold on the victim's face for a beat before moving on.
- **"Everyone thought I'd ___. I didn't."** On that line, flash what everyone expected for a split second (a ghosted or thought-bubble version is fine), then cut it off and show what he really did.
- **Pace:** quick and bouncy for the gag run, with a new picture every one to two lines. Slow right down only on the beats marked HOLD.
- **Light and colour:** bright, sunny and saturated, like a toy box. Even dungeons and ruined streets are colourful and easy to read. Nothing gloomy, no fog, no horror.
- **Sound:** light and playful music with a sneaky bounce (plucked strings, a tuba or bassoon, soft percussion), not epic drums. Cartoon-clean sound effects on each gag (pop, boing, splash, honk). Drop the music to near silence for the deadpan one-liners so they land, then bring it back.
- **Stakes stay real for one beat only.** Each story has one moment where the kid is serious. Play it straight for those few seconds (marked SERIOUS in the beats), then go back to funny.
- The house look does not change: Roblox-style blocky characters, the same caption style, the same watermark, the same end card.

**For this story:**
- The red bar is the running gag. It floats over his head in every shot, always far too long for the frame, and it barely moves however hard they hit him.
- The SERIOUS beat is the hero looking at his mom. For those four seconds drop the music, darken the sky a little and let him stand up slowly. Then straight back to funny with 'He's on dishes now.'
- The bakery fills up through the video: by the end heroes in aprons are kneading dough, a dragon is working the oven and a king is holding the door.

## Script (final, word for word, approved by Jake)
In this town, the priest reads every kid their class. Fighter. Mage. Thief. He read mine, and dropped the book. Final Boss.
A red bar appeared over my head. It was longer than our street.
And in this town, heroes get paid to clear bosses.
By lunch, four hundred of them were outside our bakery.
Everyone thought I'd fight. I took a nap. On the front step.
They hit me for an hour. I woke up once, to turn over.
Then I stayed sitting, and started charging. Two coppers a swing.
By sundown, I'd bought the bakery next door.
The king doubled the reward. So I turned myself in, and collected it.
The next day, I turned myself in again. He paid twice before he noticed.
So he sent his dragon. It looked at my red bar, and asked for an apron.
Then one hero gave up on me, and looked at my mom.
I stood up. For the first time all week.
The street cracked from our door to the castle.
He's on dishes now.
Finally the king came himself, with his biggest sword. I asked him to hold the door.
He's still holding it.
So that's who works at my mom's bakery. Four hundred heroes, a dragon, and a king. The bread is very safe.
But today, my little sister had her turn. The priest read hers, and fainted.
Mine says Final Boss. Hers says: His Boss.
Her red bar doesn't fit in the town.
