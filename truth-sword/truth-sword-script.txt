In this town, your sword grows every time you tell the truth.
I'm the stable boy, and my sword is longer than the castle bridge.
Every Sword Day, I stand at the back with my shovel.
The Prince pulled the Golden Sword. It shone so bright the crowd squinted.
He pointed it at me. "Careful, stable boy. Don't trip over your spoon."
The whole courtyard laughed, even the King.
That morning, my sword was a spoon. Rusty, too.
Then the Sword Master rang the bell for the Truth Test.
Every kid stands on the stone and says one true thing out loud.
Lie on it, and your sword shrinks in front of everyone.
Nobody has passed it clean in a hundred years.
The old story says the longest sword belongs to whoever should wear the crown.
The stone was cold as a frozen pond, and every lie cracked like a plate.
The Prince went first. "I have never cheated at anything."
Crack.
His sword shrank. And shrank. Into a toothpick.
Nobody laughed this time.
Then it was my turn.
I stepped on, and my knees were shaking.
So I said the truest thing I had. "I'm scared."
My spoon grew. Past my knees. Past my head.
Then I said, "I have never told a lie in my life."
My sword shot over the rooftops, over the bridge, through a cloud.
The Sword Master whispered, "That's a king's sword."
The whole town gasped. The King didn't.
He wasn't surprised at all.
"I knew this day would come," he said.
Because ten years ago, the King stood on it too.
He said one sentence. "I will be a good king."
His sword shrank to nothing.
He's worn an empty scabbard ever since.
"Guards!" he shouted. "Take that sword!"
A hundred guards drew, and the gates slammed shut.
I didn't swing. I just told the truth. "His scabbard is empty."
The captain looked. "It's true." His sword grew a foot.
Then every guard told the truth, and every sword grew. Except the King's.
He hid his lie for ten years. My sword found it in one sentence.
Do I take the crown? A, yes. B, no, I'm a stable boy.
