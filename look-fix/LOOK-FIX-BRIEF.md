From Claude, Mon 11-23, 11:15 PM. Jake asked for these to be worked through tonight.

Jake watched "My Class Is Dungeon Master" and "I'm the Final Boss of This Town" and said:

> I honestly don't really like either of them. I don't really like the vibe of them. They feel kind of pastel-y and really forgettable. There's not really a whole lot of personality to them.

He is right, and it can be measured. Averaged over each film:

| Film | Colour (saturation) | Brightness |
|---|---|---|
| The Phoenix Bowed to Me (he likes it) | 0.60 | 0.32 |
| The Black Knight Knelt to Me (he likes it) | 0.60 | 0.36 |

| My Footprints Stop at the River | 0.37 | 0.45 |
| I Walked Out to Meet It | 0.40 | 0.43 |

The five below are bright, flat and pale: even daylight, beige and mint sets, no shadows, a lead with a blank face, and no single picture you remember afterwards.

## What stays exactly the same

The script, the story voice file, the word timings, the captions' wording, the title and the story itself. This is a new look and a few new shots, not a new film. Reuse every set, model and animation you can.

## The house look from now on (all five, and every later build)

1. **Deep, saturated colour.** Aim for the Phoenix: an average saturation near 0.55 to 0.60. Royal blue, deep red, gold, emerald. No mint, peach, beige or baby blue as a main colour.
2. **One strong light, real shadows.** A low sun, a moon, a fire, a lantern, a glow. Dark areas should be properly dark so the characters stand out. No flat, even daylight.
3. **The lead stands out in every shot:** one bright, saturated outfit colour that nothing else in the film uses, and light on the face.
4. **One picture people remember**, and it is on frame 0 with the title. Each film's is named below.
5. **A lead with a personality.** Bigger faces: brows, eyes, mouth. Each lead gets one attitude, named below, and holds it. Hold the reactions of the people around them a beat longer; that is where the jokes land.
6. **A world that is lived in:** people, props, signs, smoke, birds. No empty flat ground.
7. **Jake's camera rule:** steady shots joined by hard cuts for the opening (about 25 seconds), then smooth gliding camera. No whips, no jerks.
8. **Sound:** scene effects, no looping music bed.
9. **The new sponsor ending** (`sponsor-ending/SPONSOR-ENDING-V2.md` and its voice file on this branch): the lead pulls out a phone inside the last scene. It replaces the old five-second phone card. Sponsor version and clean version, as before.

## Film by film

### dungeon-master: "My Class Is Dungeon Master" (about 54 s of story)
- **The picture to remember (frame 0):** the castle courtyard at night has split open, and a huge glowing dungeon drops away beneath the castle, level after level, torches all the way down. The kid stands at the edge in a deep purple hooded cloak with a rune book open in his hands, its light on his face from below. The cracked class stone smokes behind him.
- **Palette:** night blue stone, purple and gold, torch orange from below.
- **The lead:** smug and deadpan. He never hurries and never raises his hands; he turns a page and things move. One raised eyebrow.
- **Make these moments big:** the stone cracking in half; his brother arriving in a bucket; "Floor 9 is now floor 1" (whole floors sliding like drawers, seen in cut-away); the dragon swapped for a goose and back; the stairs turning into a slide; the dungeon door appearing in the king's bedroom; the prince sweeping badly, in his armour.
- **Last picture:** the whole castle lifting off its hill on a block of dungeon, the kid reading, not looking.

### final-boss: "I'm the Final Boss of This Town" (about 64 s of story)
- **The picture to remember (frame 0):** a red boss health bar stretched right across the top of the screen with FINAL BOSS on it, over a small kid asleep on a bakery step, while four hundred armoured heroes with tiny bars fill the street. Dusk-red sky.
- **Palette:** red and black bar, warm bakery gold, a dusk sky going purple, steel-blue armour.
- **The lead:** bored. Eyes half shut, eating a bread roll. He stands up exactly once.
- **Make these moments big:** the bar unrolling down the street, longer than the street; heroes hitting him while he naps and turns over; the coin jar filling ("two coppers a swing"); the dragon reading the bar and tying on an apron; **the one time he stands** (the hero looks at his mom): the light drops, the bar pulses, the street cracks from the door to the castle. Give that the most care in the film; the king holding the bakery door with his biggest sword on his back.
- **Last picture:** his little sister's bar, so long it runs off both sides of the screen and over the rooftops, him looking up at it, finally awake.

### zombie-cure: "The Zombies Run From Me" (about 64 s of story)
- **The picture to remember (frame 0):** a hundred zombies sprinting AWAY down a city street, looking back in terror at one small kid in a bright red hoodie holding his hand up for a high five. A hand-painted sign on a wall: BEWARE OF KID.
- **Palette:** this is the palest of the five; push it hardest. A city at golden hour going to neon dusk: deep teal shadows, orange sun, pink and yellow shop signs, the ice cream truck lit up like a jukebox. Zombies a strong green with pale glowing eyes. Mom's raincoat a bright yellow.
- **The lead:** cheerful and relentless. He thinks this is a game of tag and cannot understand why nobody wants to play.
- **Make these moments big:** the zombie biting his arm, making a face and apologising; the pillow left in the glass box; the truck's music pulling a crowd; the high-five line (baker, bus driver, maths teacher last); the zombies working it out and running; the wall and the sign; him arriving over the wall in a parcel; the hug at the river, played straight and warm; four thousand people walking home behind them.
- **Last picture:** Gary, grey and unbothered, getting a tenth high five.

### footprints: "My Footprints Stop at the River" (about 37 s of story)
- **The picture to remember (frame 0):** blue hour. A trail of GLOWING footprints, cyan-white like moonlight on water, runs from a small front door down a dark lane to a black river and stops at the edge. The kid stands in the doorway in a mustard-yellow jumper looking down at them, his face lit from below by the glow.
- **Palette:** deep blue dusk and night from the first shot (no beige daytime interiors), warm yellow windows, the footprints as the only cold light. They should glow and pulse faintly.
- **The lead:** careful and worried, a kid who has never gone anywhere. Wide eyes, small movements.
- **Make these moments big:** the baker's prints going to the pub while he "acts surprised" (the one joke, keep it warm and lit by the pub's windows); the locked door and the bed; the look out of the window; **the second set**: bigger, bare, wet, coming up out of the river to his door, each one landing with a soft sound as the camera glides along them; and the last line: his prints stop being steps and become two long drag lines down to the water. Hold on that.
- **Last picture:** the two lines disappearing into the black river, one ripple.
- This one is suspense. Do not make it bright. Make it dark with strong, clear light sources.

### i-walked-out-to-meet-it: "I Walked Out to Meet It" (about 2:30 of story)
- **The picture to remember (frame 0):** deep blue night, snow, a full moon. A small boy in a red scarf holds up one orange lantern in front of a creature three times his height: long thin arms, black body, a white mask with no face, tilted toward him. His shadow runs back across the snow toward the town.
- **Palette:** it is already close. Push it: deeper blue-black night, the lantern as the only warm thing in the film, bright moon rim on the creature and the trees, the mask a clean white. The town's windows warm yellow until the shutters slam.
- **The lead:** frightened and walking anyway. Let us see the fear: breath, shaking lantern, a swallow. That is the whole story.
- **Make these moments big:** "Scared kids still show up" in the square (hold the king's face); the hundred steps, counted, with the prints and the long shadow; the shutters slamming one after another; the head tilting at fifty; the lantern going out at twenty, so the film drops to moonlight only; the breathing at ten; the scream from behind and the girl running across the snow. Keep the camera slow the whole way; the fear is in how long it takes.
- Falling snow and breath clouds throughout. The creature should be taller and stiller than it is now.
