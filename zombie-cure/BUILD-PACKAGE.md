# Build package: THE ZOMBIES RUN FROM ME

Build this one third, after dungeon-master and final-boss. It needs a modern city, so it will take longer. Target: posted by Thursday 11-26.

Channel: "In this town" story reels for Idle Merchant RNG (@idlemerchantrng) on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube. The audience is mostly young adult men who like fantasy and games. Format: vertical 1080x1920, 30 fps, Roblox-style blocky 3D characters, bold burned-in captions.

**About this story:** it grants a wish. The viewer should want to be this kid: in a zombie city he is the one person who cannot be turned, and one touch from him turns a zombie back. He treats the end of the world like recess. Jake picked this idea and approved every word by voice on 11-22. It is short on purpose (about a minute) and has no question or vote at the end.

## The vibe (new section, asked for by Jake on 11-22: read this before anything else)
Jake's words for this style: **"never let them know your next move."** It is deadpan comedy about a kid with far too much power.
- **The kid is never surprised, never angry and never in a hurry.** Small calm face, half-closed eyes, hands in pockets or doing something ordinary (sweeping, yawning, eating). He does not pose, flex or shout. The less he reacts, the funnier and the more powerful he looks.
- **Everyone else overreacts.** Kings, princes, heroes and zombies get the big faces: jaws dropping, eyes popping, arms flailing, running in circles. All the energy in the frame comes from them.
- **Joke timing: say it, then show it.** Each joke is two beats: the setup picture, then a quick snap (under half a second) to the surprise picture exactly as the punchline word is spoken. Never show the surprise before the word. Then hold on the victim's face for a beat before moving on.
- **"Everyone thought I'd ___. I didn't."** On that line, flash what everyone expected for a split second (a ghosted or thought-bubble version is fine), then cut it off and show what he really did.
- **Pace:** quick and bouncy for the gag run, with a new picture every one to two lines. Slow right down only on the beats marked HOLD.
- **Light and colour:** bright, sunny and saturated, like a toy box. Even dungeons and ruined streets are colourful and easy to read. Nothing gloomy, no fog, no horror.
- **Sound:** light and playful music with a sneaky bounce (plucked strings, a tuba or bassoon, soft percussion), not epic drums. Cartoon-clean sound effects on each gag (pop, boing, splash, honk). Drop the music to near silence for the deadpan one-liners so they land, then bring it back.
- **Stakes stay real for one beat only.** Each story has one moment where the kid is serious. Play it straight for those few seconds (marked SERIOUS in the beats), then go back to funny.
- The house look does not change: Roblox-style blocky characters, the same caption style, the same watermark, the same end card.

**For this story:**
- **These are friendly cartoon zombies.** Green skin, round eyes, torn but clean clothes, arms out, shuffling. No blood, no wounds, no bones, nothing scary. The bite at the start is a gentle chomp on a sleeve, and the zombie looks sorry about it.
- When he touches a zombie: a pop, a puff of green smoke, and a normal person stands there blinking.
- The world is a sunny modern city gone a bit messy: abandoned cars, shopping trolleys, traffic lights. Bright sky, bright colours.
- The SERIOUS beat is the hug with his mom at the river. Play it warm and straight for about five seconds, music soft. Then 'Why aren't you in the bunker?' brings the comedy back.
- Gary gets the last laugh: a completely ordinary, slightly grey, tired-looking man holding a coffee cup among the last abandoned cars.

## Script (final, word for word, approved by Jake)
In this city, one bite turns you into a zombie. I'm the only kid who's immune. And whoever I touch turns back.
I found out by accident. A zombie bit my arm, made a face, and said, "Sorry about that."
The grown-ups said, "You're the cure. Stay in the bunker." They put me in a glass box.
I left a pillow in it.
Everyone thought I'd sneak out. I didn't. I took the ice cream truck. Zombies love the music.
A hundred came running. I held out my hand for a high five.
The baker. The bus driver. My math teacher. I did him last.
Then I got bored of high fives. So I switched to tag. Then dodgeball.
That's when the zombies figured it out. And they started running from me.
Turns out they like being zombies. No school.
They built a wall to keep me out. They put up a sign. Beware of kid.
I'm ten, and I'm the scariest thing in the city.
So I mailed myself over the wall in a box.
The zombie in charge wore a yellow raincoat. My mom was always good at organizing.
She ran. I caught her at the river. I didn't tag her. I hugged her.
She blinked, and said, "Why aren't you in the bunker?"
We walked home. Four thousand people walked behind us.
There's one zombie left. I've high fived him nine times. Nothing happens.
Turns out he's not a zombie. That's just Gary.
