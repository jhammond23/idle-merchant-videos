In this town, the king's dragon takes one kid every winter. This year, it picked me. Everyone told me to run. I hatched an army instead.
Every kid in town gets an egg on their tenth birthday. Most hatch a lizard the size of a shoe. The mayor's son got a hawk, and he hasn't shut up about it since.
My egg sat in the window for a year. Cold. Kids called it a rock.
This morning, it cracked down the middle. Out stepped a bear made of stone, taller than the castle gate. Then a fox made of smoke, with eyes like lanterns.
The whole square went silent.
Then every lizard in town crawled out of every pocket and lined up behind me. The mayor's son's hawk flew off his arm and landed on my shoulder.
At noon, forty knights rode in to take my egg. The captain pointed his lance at me and said, "Hand it over, boy."
The bear stood up. Its shadow covered the whole square. The fox circled the knights once, and their war hawks left them too. Every one of them flew to me.
The knights rode home with empty arms. Up on his balcony, the king watched. He didn't say a word. He just went pale.
But tonight, the egg went dim. It only has fire for one.
The bear can hold a dragon down. The fox can steal its fire.
