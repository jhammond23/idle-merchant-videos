Channel: "In this town" story reels for Idle Merchant RNG (@idlemerchantrng) on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube. Format: vertical 1080x1920, 30 fps, Roblox-style blocky 3D characters, bold burned-in captions.

**This batch is a style test (Jake, 11-22).** The biggest "in this town" accounts were studied this morning, and these scripts follow what their hits share. So this package differs from earlier ones in five ways:
1. **No Fold.** The lead is an ordinary kid.
2. **No question and no A/B vote at the end.** The story ends on its last line, which is a twist.
3. **The voice is faster** (about 3.9 words a second). It is final. Do not speed it up again.
4. **Shot length must vary.** See "The look".
5. **Title-only caption** when it is posted (Claude handles posting).

**About this story:** A dark-funny town-rule story. The tax is one memory a year and the tax man chooses which. The narrator is a kid who never forgets anything. He beats the tax man with the rule's own logic, and then the last two lines show what it cost him.

## The look
- **Vary the shot length. Do not cut on a fixed beat.** Jake's note: the successful videos have no set time between scenes. Use quick cuts of about 1 second for lists, jokes and action, and hold for 3 to 4 seconds on the big pictures (marked HOLD in the beats). Never hold a still frame: on a hold, the camera drifts or pushes in.
- Clear, readable light in every shot. Sunset and night scenes are allowed here, but they must be warm or moonlit and sharp: faces and the key object always clearly lit. No fog and no murky grade.
- Huge captions: white capitals with a thick black outline, 2-3 words at a time, centered low, matching the voice word for word. The last two lines are spoken quietly, so their captions matter most: show every word.
- The @idlemerchantrng watermark sits in a CORNER for the whole video.
- Music: low and uneasy under the voice, and it should drop out for the last line.
- **End card:** after the last line, hold the final picture for about one second, then end with the standard 5-second "Use Flashloop.AI, creator code RNG" card, the same one that closes my-rock-hatched-a-dragon. That is the only promo allowed. Nothing promotional before it.
- Kid-safe: nobody is shown being hurt, no blood, no body. The scares are what we do not see.

## The lead: Ned
Ned is an ordinary kid, just turned ten, plain clothes. He carries a fat little diary and a pencil everywhere and writes in it constantly. Build him from the Lunchbox cast parts. Do not use Fold in this video.

## Script (final, word for word)
In this town, the tax is one memory a year, and the tax man gets to pick. My dad doesn't know my name anymore. On Thursday, it's my turn to pay.
He puts two fingers on your forehead and reaches for the best thing in there. Whatever he touches, he keeps.
Old Mr. Finch paid with his wedding. He's been a lot happier.
The cook at the inn paid with her soup recipe. She's been guessing ever since.
And the tax man keeps them. All of them. He's had four hundred first kisses, and none of them were his.
Last year he took me, out of my dad. Dad's very polite to me now. He calls me "young man."
You start paying at ten. I turned ten on Sunday.
My name is Ned, and I'm the kid who never forgets. Ask me what I had for lunch on any day of my life.
So I've got a lot he'd like. My mom singing. The time it snowed in June. My dog, when he was small.
On Monday, the tax man stopped at our gate and looked up at my window. He didn't knock.
On Tuesday, I watched him do the widow next door, through her window. She was thinking about her cat. You could tell. He took the cat. She still feeds it. She doesn't know why.
That's when I saw it. He doesn't dig. He takes whatever is on top.
So on Wednesday night, I did not sleep. I picked one thing, and I wrote it in my diary, so I'd know what I lost. Then I sat up in bed and thought about it, as hard as I could, until morning.
Thursday. He put two cold fingers on my forehead and smiled.
Then he stopped smiling.
There was only one thing on top. It was him. His fingers on my dad's head. His smile. And my dad's face, afterward, looking at me like I'd come to the wrong house.
He had to take it. He'd already touched it.
I don't think he had ever seen himself before.
He walked out very slowly. At the door, he patted me on the head. Two fingers. A little too long. I thought that was strange.
He hasn't collected since. They say he sits in his house with the curtains shut.
Dad still calls me young man. I tell him my name every morning. He says, "Good name."
And I kept everything else. I checked. I write it all down, just in case.
My diary says I have never forgotten a thing.
It also says I have a sister.
There's a girl at our table every morning. I've been calling her "miss."

## Other characters and things
- **The tax man:** tall, thin, dark coat, a huge ledger under one arm. Always holds up two long fingers. Polite, hungry smile.
- **Memories:** show every memory as a small glowing picture card floating over a person's head. When the tax man touches a forehead with two fingers, one card slides out, he holds it up, and tucks it into his ledger. The person's face goes blank for a second.
- **Dad:** kind, tired, plain clothes. He is warm and polite to Ned the way you are with a neighbour's child. He tips his hat to him.
- **The widow next door:** an old woman with a cat. After the tax man's visit she still puts a bowl down, then looks at the cat as if she has never seen it.
- **The girl:** about eight, sits at Ned's family table at every breakfast from the very first family shot. Never explained until the last line. She must be clearly visible in at least two early shots so the ending pays off.
