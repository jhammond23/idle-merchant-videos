In this town, every kid gets one magic seed.
Every sprout gets measured. That's how strong it is.
A bush is fifty. Our champion's oak is nine thousand.
Mine's a dot. A one.
It's broccoli for ants.
And on Harvest Day, a rock troll came stomping.
The troll didn't stop. It just squished the pie table.
I had one idea. "Hug him first," I whispered.
My dot shot up.
It grabbed the troll. Gently. Like a teddy.
Nobody cheered. Nobody moved.
Then my tree got measured. Nine thousand and one.
A tiny seed grows when it's needed.
It grew branches. Then blossoms. Then golden apples.
The champion stared at his oak. Ten years, it was the tallest.
Every bush in town leaned toward my tree.
I pointed at the broken tables. My tree fixed them.
Then it handed me an apple. From across the square.
So the champion made me an offer. All his gold. For my tree.
A tree. Or a mountain of gold. Which would you pick?
I kept my tree. "But the troll can go," I said. "Let go."
Out rolled the troll. Giggling like a baby. Not one grump left.
A tree hug squeezes out all the grumpy.
Then the old garden keeper grabbed my arm. She was shaking.
"Tiny seeds don't listen," she said. "Not to anyone. Not in a thousand years."
"It hugged when you said hug. It let go when you said so."
A tiny seed can grow into anything. It just never does. That's why it's a one.
Mine does. For me.
Nobody ever woke a tiny seed. Until today.
Then the giggling troll sniffled. One rumble.
And a shadow covered the sun.
Something stomped onto the hill. The hill looked small.
Its mother. Nine hundred thousand.
Nine thousand and one was a pebble.
Everybody ran. My tree didn't.
It bent its branches around me. And it looked down.
It was waiting for one word. Would you say "hug"?
