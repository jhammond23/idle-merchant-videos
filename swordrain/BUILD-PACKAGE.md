## Script (final, word for word)
In this town, it rains swords once a year. You keep the one that lands next to you.
A sword only moves for its owner. The blacksmith can't lift his own kid's dagger.
I'm Fold. Mine never landed.
It's been falling for four years. Every year, it's bigger.
Tomorrow at noon, it finally gets here. And it's bigger than the castle.
A sword never hits its owner. It lands right next to him.
So wherever I'm standing tomorrow gets a castle dropped on it.
My whole street moved away from me. Everyone but my sister.
Nell said, "If it lands, it lands on both of us."
The king had a better idea.
He rode me to the next kingdom, and stood me at their front gate.
He said, "You're not a boy. You're how I aim."
There were kids on their wall. One of them waved at me.
At noon, the sky went dark. I could hear it whistling.
And the guards let go of me. All of them.
For one minute, I was the most dangerous thing in two kingdoms.
So I walked toward the king.
His whole army got out of my way.
I started running. He ran faster.
But I wasn't dropping a castle on anybody.
I stopped in the empty field, halfway between his army and their wall.
Then somebody grabbed my right hand.
Nell. She'd hidden in the supply wagon. "I said both of us."
It landed on my left. The wind knocked us flat.
When the dust cleared, a wall of steel stood between two kingdoms, taller than both castles.
The king wanted it. He sent ten thousand men to drag it out.
It didn't move an inch.
I put one hand on it, and I lifted.
Then a new shadow fell over both kingdoms.
A sword that big fell from a hand that big.
Nobody here ever asked who drops them.
This one had fingers.
Do I keep it, or give it back? A, keep it. B, give it back.
