## The vibe (new section, asked for by Jake on 11-22: read this before anything else)
Jake's words for this style: **"never let them know your next move."** It is deadpan comedy about a kid with far too much power.
- **The kid is never surprised, never angry and never in a hurry.** Small calm face, half-closed eyes, hands in pockets or doing something ordinary (sweeping, yawning, eating). He does not pose, flex or shout. The less he reacts, the funnier and the more powerful he looks.
- **Everyone else overreacts.** Kings, princes, heroes and zombies get the big faces: jaws dropping, eyes popping, arms flailing, running in circles. All the energy in the frame comes from them.
- **Joke timing: say it, then show it.** Each joke is two beats: the setup picture, then a quick snap (under half a second) to the surprise picture exactly as the punchline word is spoken. Never show the surprise before the word. Then hold on the victim's face for a beat before moving on.
- **"Everyone thought I'd ___. I didn't."** On that line, flash what everyone expected for a split second (a ghosted or thought-bubble version is fine), then cut it off and show what he really did.
- **Pace:** quick and bouncy for the gag run, with a new picture every one to two lines. Slow right down only on the beats marked HOLD.
- **Light and colour:** bright, sunny and saturated, like a toy box. Even dungeons and ruined streets are colourful and easy to read. Nothing gloomy, no fog, no horror.
- **Sound:** light and playful music with a sneaky bounce (plucked strings, a tuba or bassoon, soft percussion), not epic drums. Cartoon-clean sound effects on each gag (pop, boing, splash, honk). Drop the music to near silence for the deadpan one-liners so they land, then bring it back.
- **Stakes stay real for one beat only.** Each story has one moment where the kid is serious. Play it straight for those few seconds (marked SERIOUS in the beats), then go back to funny.
- The house look does not change: Roblox-style blocky characters, the same caption style, the same watermark, the same end card.

**For this story:**
- This story's serious beat is tiny: the prince taking the little brother. The kid's eyes narrow for one second, and that is all.
- The dungeon itself is his sidekick. Walls slide, floors swap and doors walk away the instant he asks, with a soft stone rumble and a little dust. Treat the dungeon like a friendly pet.
- Nobody is hurt. The prince and his party end up wet, dizzy and embarrassed.

## Script (final, word for word, approved by Jake)
In this town, a stone gives every kid a class. Most get Farmer. The prince got Paladin. Mine said Dungeon Master, and then the stone cracked in half.
The king laughed, and handed me a broom. Dungeon steps.
That night, the prince marched his party in to prove I was nothing. He took my little brother to carry the torches.
Everyone thought I'd run in after them. I didn't. I asked the dungeon to send my brother up. He arrived in a bucket.
Then I rearranged things. Floor nine is now floor one. The exit is now a pond.
The prince drew his sword on the dragon. I'd already swapped the dragon for a goose.
He laughed. So I put the dragon back. Behind him.
He ran for the stairs. I made the stairs a slide. Going down.
The king sent his army to arrest me. So I moved the dungeon door. It's in his bedroom now.
He offered me gold. I said no. He offered me the crown. I said no.
I asked for one thing. The prince sweeps the steps.
He's terrible at it.
A Paladin gets a castle. I got everything under it.
And tonight, I'm moving the castle. I haven't told it where.
