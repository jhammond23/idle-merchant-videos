In this town, wars are fought by war beasts, and the winner keeps the loser's crown.
This morning, the enemy's war stag knelt to me. So their king woke up a mountain.
It was his first war beast, and its antlers held nine thousand crowns.
My stag stepped in front of me. I walked around him.
The mountain raised one hoof over my head. Their whole army ran. I didn't.
The hoof stopped. Then the mountain knelt. To me.
Nine thousand crowns came down around me like golden hail.
The king screamed, "Get up! Crush him!" The mountain didn't move.
So I looked up at the king and said, "Your turn. Come stand where I'm standing."
He wouldn't come out from behind his wall. In nine thousand wars, he had never once stood in front of a beast.
That's when I understood. A war beast only kneels to its true king, and a true king is just the one who doesn't run.
The mountain laid its neck down like a road, and I walked up. From the top, I could see every town he ever took.
"You're a kid with a goat," the king yelled. "You can't rule nine thousand towns!"
He was right. So I didn't.
The winner keeps the loser's crown. I won. That made them mine to give back.
The two stags carried them home, one crown to every town.
By sunset, every town had come to take its throne back. They pulled the king's castle apart, one chair at a time.
The king was left standing in an empty field, with nowhere to sit.
A crown you take makes a town afraid of you. A crown you give back makes it follow you.
I kept one crown. Our goat wears it. He thinks he won the war.
Would you have given them back? A, give them back. B, keep all nine thousand.
Follow if you wouldn't have run.
