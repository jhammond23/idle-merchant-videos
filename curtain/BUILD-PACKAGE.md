## Script (final, word for word)
In this town, the king sells the sunlight.
If you can't pay, he hangs a curtain over your street.
My street has been dark for six years.
I'm Fold, and I pulled the whole curtain down.
My little sister Nell planted one sunflower, and it wouldn't open.
So I climbed our roof with a broom, and poked one hole in the curtain.
One sunbeam landed right on her flower.
It opened like a yawn.
A gold thread came loose from the hole, and Nell grabbed it.
I said, "Don't pull that."
Then the baker held up a spoon.
The light bounced to the next house, and the next.
Spoons, pans, a tuba.
One sunbeam went down the whole street.
The king saw it from his castle.
He said, "Patch that hole, and charge them triple."
His men climbed up with needles as long as spears.
I could poke more holes, but they'd patch every one.
So I grabbed the gold thread, and I ran.
The curtain came apart behind me, row by row, like a sweater.
Sunlight poured into every window.
People came outside and just stood there, with their eyes closed.
Nell's sunflower turned its head to watch me run.
The king yelled, "That's my sun!"
I said, "You can't sell the sky."
Then the last of the curtain fell.
And behind it, on the mountain, something huge was asleep.
The sunlight touched its face.
The king whispered, "That curtain wasn't for you. It was for him."
One giant eye started to open.
Do we hang the curtain back up? A, hang it. B, let him wake.
Follow if you'd have pulled the thread.
