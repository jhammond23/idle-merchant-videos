## Why it looks the way it does (from the 11-21 analysis of what went viral)
Our biggest hits (My Reflection Knelt to Me at 840K, A Giant Knelt to Me at 355K) opened on ONE huge character with a face and a crown, bowing to a tiny kid, in bright daylight in front of a crowd. The recent flops were dark and foggy, used small captions, and held one still shot for a long time. So:
- Bright noon daylight with a blue sky in every outdoor shot. No fog, no dusk, no dark grade.
- A new shot about every 1.5 s. No shot held longer than 3.5 s.
- Huge captions: white capitals with a thick black outline, 2-3 words at a time, centered low, matching the voice word for word.

## Script (final, word for word)
In this town, whoever pulls the sword out of the stone becomes king.
Nobody has done it in a thousand years.
I'm Rowan, and I didn't pull out the sword.
I pulled out the whole stone.
This year, the king made every grown-up try.
If you failed, you went to his quarry.
When it was my dad's turn, I went instead.
I pulled. The sword didn't move.
But the ground did.
The square cracked like an eggshell.
And the stone opened its eyes.
It was a giant's head.
The sword was stuck in his crown.
He stood up, taller than the castle.
Then he bowed to me.
The prince once made the sword wiggle.
He still talks about it.
Today, he didn't say a word.
The king yelled, "That doesn't count! You pulled out a head!"
I said, "Call it a head start."
So the giant pulled out the sword himself.
He handed it to me.
The king screamed, "Seize him!"
Every guard in the square knelt instead.
The giant picked up the king by his cape.
He set him on the bell tower, like a cat on a fridge.
For twenty years, Dad paid a gold coin to try.
Every year, he lost.
He always said, "If I win, you'll never be hungry again."
The whole town called him the Coin Man.
The giant lifted the castle roof like a lid.
He shook out the king's gold.
Exactly twenty coins rolled to Dad's feet.
Then the giant knelt to Dad.
"Twenty years," he said. "I felt every pull."
"You never pulled to be king. You pulled for your son."
"Today, your son pulled for you."
"In a thousand years, you're the only two who pulled for someone else."
Dad couldn't talk.
He just hugged me.
He was crying. The good kind.
Then the giant looked at the mountains.
"There are four more swords," he said.
"I was the smallest."
Do we wake the others? A, wake them. B, let them sleep.
Follow if you'd pull for someone you love.

## Characters
- **Rowan (me, the narrator):** a boy of about 12 in a plain brown tunic, small and calm.
- **Dad:** a tired, kind man in a worn work shirt, with big hands. He holds one gold coin.
- **The king:** cruel and smug, with a crown and a red and gold robe, up on his balcony over the square.
- **The prince:** about 14, smug, in gold and red.
- **The Giant:** a giant king made of mossy gray stone with a long stone beard, a gold-veined stone crown with an old sword stuck in the top of it, and glowing gold eyes. He's taller than the castle. He must look regal and kind, NOT like a rock monster. It's important that he looks clearly different from the stone giant in our recent "100-Year Giant" video: this one is a crowned, bearded king.
- **Guards** in silver armor, and a packed crowd in the square.
