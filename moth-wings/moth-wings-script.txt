In this town, every kid grows wings on their tenth birthday.
The bigger your wings, the higher you rank.
Sparrow wings are weak. Eagle wings are strong. Griffin wings, one kid in a thousand, rule the sky.
But on my tenth birthday, I grew moth wings. Grey, and the size of my hands.
The whole square laughed before they'd even opened.
For two years, even the little kids flicked them for fun.
But whenever someone smaller got picked on, I stepped in. I knew exactly how the bottom felt.
Then at the Sky Festival, the duke's son cornered a first-year at the edge of the cliff.
His wings were griffin gold, wider than a wagon.
I stepped between them.
The whole cliff burst out laughing. "A moth is going to stop a griffin?"
He grinned and dove straight at me.
An arm's length from my face, he stopped. Frozen in midair.
Nobody understood why.
Then every pair of wings on that cliff folded, and every kid dropped to one knee.
And my wings opened. All the way. They blocked out the sun.
