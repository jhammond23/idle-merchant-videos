## The rules for the cut section (how a professional editor holds attention)

1. **The camera is never still.** Every shot does exactly one of these: a slow push-in, a slow sideways slide past something close to the lens, a slow tilt or crane up to reveal size, or the subject moving toward or past the camera. A push-in is about 5-10% closer per second.
2. **Shots are 1.2 to 3 seconds long.** Cut on the stressed word, or on a movement (a head turns, a hoof lands, a hand points: cut in the middle of the action).
3. **Change the shot size at every cut:** wide, then close, then extreme close, then wide. Never two cuts in a row at the same size or angle.
4. **Punch-in cuts on the key words** (listed per film): a straight cut from a wider shot to a much closer shot on the same line of sight, landing exactly on the word. It should feel like a drum hit.
5. **Something in the foreground** in most shots: grass, banners, spears, pillars, candles, the backs of heads. That gives depth when the camera moves.
6. **Creatures from low down:** the camera at grass or knee height looking up. **The kid at eye level**, face lit, close.
7. **Reaction faces:** short close-ups of people reacting (laughing, shocked, scared). Faces hold viewers.
8. **The last shot of the section moves in the same direction as the long glide that follows**, so the hand-off into the glide is invisible.
